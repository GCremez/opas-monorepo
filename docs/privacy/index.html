To explain your letters, Opas has to read them. This policy explains exactly what happens to them, in the same plain words Opas uses for everything else.

  • We ask before the AI reads any letter, and name health information plainly.
  • Names, ID numbers and addresses are removed first. We may not catch every one.

  • Delete a letter, or your whole account, any time.

Who is responsible

Salau Distributions (Y-tunnus 3244824-8), Finland, is responsible for your data: the "data controller" in the GDPR. Questions about your data go to privacy@getopas.app.

Opas is an independent app. It is not run by, or connected to, Kela, Vero (the Finnish Tax Administration), Migri or any other Finnish authority.

What we collect, and why

The GDPR says we need a reason, called a legal basis, for every kind of data. We use these:

  • Your account: we need it to give you the service.
  • Your consent: you said yes, and you can take it back.
  • Explicit consent: for health and other sensitive information in your letters (see below).
  • Legitimate interest: keeping the app working, in ways you would expect.

How a letter is read

When you scan a letter, Opas first reads the text. Photos are read by Amazon Textract, in Frankfurt, Germany. PDFs are read on our own server.

Then Opas removes names, personal identity codes, addresses, phone numbers and bank account numbers. This happens on our own server, before any AI sees the letter. We may not catch every one, so some personal details can still be in the text.

The rest of the text goes to Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, which runs on Amazon Bedrock in Stockholm, Sweden, so your letter stays in the EU. It writes the explanation you see. Neither Anthropic nor Amazon uses your letters to train AI.

We ask your permission before the first scan. To take it back, tap You, then AI reading, in the app. Opas stops reading new letters for your account, and asks you again if you scan another one. Letters already read stay as they are until you delete them. You can also email privacy@getopas.app and we'll turn it off for you.

Health and other sensitive information

Some letters show sensitive things. A Kela decision can mention an illness or a disability, for example. The GDPR calls this "special category" data and protects it more strictly.

Opas only processes it to explain your letter, and only with your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)). The consent screen before your first scan names health information, and tapping Allow and continue is that consent. We save the date and the version of the wording you agreed to. If the wording changes, we ask you again.

Opas doesn't use this information for anything else, doesn't build a profile from it, and never shares it except with the services below that process your letters for us.

How long we keep it

  • Letters and your account: until you delete them. Deleting a letter removes the file and its explanation.
  • Deleting your account removes everything: account, letters, files, explanations, folders, tasks and notification tokens.
  • Sign-in and reset codes expire by themselves.
  • Usage events and crash reports hold no letter content and are deleted after 12 months.

Your rights

You can ask us to show you your data, correct it, give you a copy, or delete it. You can also object to what we do on legitimate interest, and take back your consent (in the app: You, then AI reading). For everything else, email privacy@getopas.app. We answer within one month.

You can delete your account in the app, or without the app.

If you think we've handled your data wrongly, you can complain to the Data Protection Ombudsman of Finland at tietosuoja.fi.

How we keep it safe

Everything travels encrypted (HTTPS). Your files are in private storage that only Opas can reach, and download links stop working after an hour. Your sign-in stays in your phone's secure storage.

Children

Opas is made for adults handling their own official letters. It is not meant for children under 16.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that matters to you, we'll tell you in the app before the change applies. The date at the top shows when it last changed.
